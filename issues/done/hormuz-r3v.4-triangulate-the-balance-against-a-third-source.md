# Triangulate the global balance against a third independent source

## Description

The 308.171 mb unreconciled residual is currently the difference between exactly two
systems: EIA's supply-demand balance and a project composite of IEA observed-stock
headlines. With only two sources, the residual has no error bars — there is no way to tell
whether EIA's supply is too low, EIA's demand is too high, or the IEA composite is too small.

A third independent balance would bound the residual rather than merely naming it.

The IEA composite is also weaker than it looks and should be rebuilt. It mixes the latest
explicit monthly value from different OMR vintages, and those preliminary months were
revised heavily: March 85 to 129, April 117 to 74, May 143 to 73. A single internally
consistent vintage would be a better comparator even if it is less current.

## Acceptance Criteria

- OPEC MOMR monthly supply, demand and OECD stock series extracted for February-July 2026
  on a documented scope basis.
- A same-vintage IEA balance used as the comparator wherever the OMR publishes one, replacing
  the mixed-vintage composite, with the composite retained only as a sensitivity.
- A three-way comparison table of implied stock change: EIA balance, IEA balance, OPEC
  balance, with scope and definitional differences stated (all-liquids versus crude, OECD
  versus global, processing gain treatment, biofuels).
- A defensible **range** for the unreconciled residual replacing the current point estimate,
  propagated into the r3v.1 tier tally so the T4 bucket carries a low/base/high.
- Explicit note that agency balances are not independent in the strict sense: they share
  underlying country submissions and often revise toward each other.

## Verdict

The statement that the **308.171 million barrel March-June residual is normal is not
defensible**. March-June contains **122 days**, so the point is **2.526 mb/d**, not a generic
"roughly 300 mb over several months". It is above all six documented annual interagency
balance ranges in the bounded historical sample and roughly **1.94 times** the largest one.
Call it **exceptional in this benchmark**, while noting that exceptional does not mean
unprecedented for preliminary quarterly data: IEF reported a still-forecast 4Q23 agency
balance divergence of **2.6 mb/d**.

This result does **not** prove that 308.171 million physical barrels are hidden. It proves
that the cross-system plug is too large to wave away as ordinary statistical noise without
showing definitions and vintage sensitivity.

## OPEC February-July extraction

The MOMR series was visually checked against the rendered July stock and balance pages and
transcribed from Tables 9-1 and 10-1. OPEC's latest OECD commercial-stock observation lags
each report by roughly two months:

| MOMR | latest stock month | OECD commercial stocks, mb | m/m, mb | 1Q26 world demand, mb/d | 2Q26 world demand, mb/d | 1Q/2Q call on DoC, mb/d |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| February | Dec-25 | 2,845 | +6.5 | 105.6 | 105.6 | 42.6 / 42.2 |
| March | Jan-26 | 2,824 | -19.9 | 105.6 | 105.6 | 42.6 / 42.2 |
| April | Feb-26 | 2,826 | +6.2 | 105.7 | 105.1 | 42.8 / 41.8 |
| May | Mar-26 | 2,774 | -21.6 | 106.1 | 104.6 | 43.1 / 41.4 |
| June | Apr-26 | 2,748 | -48.4 | 105.9 | 104.2 | 42.9 / 41.0 |
| July | May-26 | 2,770 | -21.8 | 106.0 | 103.7 | 42.8 / 40.7 |

The July MOMR adds DoC crude production of **39.9 mb/d in 1Q26** and **34.3 mb/d in
2Q26**, producing stated balances of **-2.9** and **-6.4 mb/d**. Applying the 1Q rate only
to March and the 2Q rate to April-June implies a **672.3 million barrel** March-June draw.
The March allocation is deliberately labelled a proxy because January-February are folded
into OPEC's 1Q average.

OPEC's OECD commercial series is useful corroboration, not a global stock comparator. It
shows a **91.8 million barrel commercial draw in March-May** in the vintages above; June is
not yet in the July MOMR. IEA's global observed series additionally includes non-OECD
stocks, government inventories and oil on water, so subtracting the two would be invalid.

## Three-way July balance comparison

**Superseded-method warning:** `hormuz-r3v.7` shows that the IEA row below is a cross-scope
call-on-DoC diagnostic, not global demand minus total supply. Do not use it as an IEA balance
or use its 261.36 mb value to define a residual range.

| system | March-June implied draw | rate | construction |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| EIA July STEO | **606.171 mb** | **4.969 mb/d** | Monthly world petroleum-and-other-liquids consumption minus supply, same July vintage. |
| IEA deprecated cross-scope diagnostic | **261.360 mb** | **2.142 mb/d** | July IEF/IEA call on DoC (38.0 mb/d 1Q, 34.2 2Q) less public IEA monthly OPEC+ production for March-June; not a total-oil balance. |
| OPEC July MOMR | **672.300 mb** | **5.511 mb/d** | July Table 10-1 1Q/2Q DoC production-demand balance, with 1Q applied to March. |

The **IEA row must not be represented as a total-oil balance or a fully same-vintage subscriber table**. The public
July IEF comparison provides the same-vintage quarterly IEA demand, non-DoC supply and call
on DoC; public OMR summaries expose one OPEC+ monthly production estimate at a time, not a
revised July monthly history. The reconstruction therefore chains the April-July public
production observations to the July call. r3v.7 retired that construction as a comparator;
the exact public Q2 demand/supply decomposition replaces it.

The disagreement is not mysterious once levels are shown. July IEF reports 2Q26 global
demand of **99.1 mb/d for IEA, 100.4 for EIA and 103.7 for OPEC**, a **4.6 mb/d** span.
OPEC's call on DoC is **40.7 mb/d**, versus **34.2 IEA** and **36.3 EIA**. Balance-model and
scope choices are therefore large enough to dominate the stock plug.

The June relaxation is preserved: IEA total OPEC+ production rises from **30.30 mb/d in
May to 32.44 in June**, and EIA total liquids supply rises from **93.48 to 97.46 mb/d**.

## Observed-stock vintage sensitivity

The public IEA summaries do not provide one fully revised July-vintage March-June table.
The ledger therefore keeps three transparent sensitivities:

- **258.6 mb draw, updated-compatible:** June's rounded 3.8 mb/d March-May draw implies
  about 206.6 mb for March-April after removing its preliminary May value; combine that
  with July's revised 73 mb May draw and 21 mb June build.
- **298.0 mb draw, existing base:** May-vintage March 129 and April 117, plus July-vintage
  May 73 and June +21. This is explicitly mixed-vintage.
- **368.0 mb draw, contemporaneous-high:** May-vintage March-April 246 plus June-vintage
  preliminary May 143, less June's later 21 mb build.

These are vintage sensitivities, not a confidence interval. Preliminary revisions are the
reason the apparent residual cannot be treated as measured physical inventory.

## Historical scale test

The most comparable public annual IEF/RFF tables report the range across agencies' net
global stock-change-and-miscellaneous balance:

| balance year | interagency range, mb/d | four-month equivalent, mb |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2015 | 0.30 | 36.6 |
| 2019 | 0.50 | 61.0 |
| 2020 | 0.94 | 114.7 |
| 2021 | 0.66 | 80.5 |
| 2022 | 0.42 | 51.2 |
| 2023 | 1.30 | 158.6 |

The current 2.526 mb/d point is above **6 of 6** observations. This is a small purposive
sample, not a fitted distribution, so the ledger reports an empirical rank rather than a
spurious p-value. Additional same-agency IEA narrative checks are also smaller: about
**0.2 mb/d in 1Q17**, **1.3 mb/d in 3Q20**, and roughly **0.7 mb/d in 2025** (observed
1.3 mb/d build versus a near-2 mb/d implied build over the first three quarters).

The 2021 table is particularly useful for scale and definitions: IEA miscellaneous-to-
balance was **-1.07 mb/d**, OPEC **-0.63**, while total global stock change plus
miscellaneous ranged from IEA **-2.33** to OPEC **-1.70** and EIA **-1.86 mb/d**. The
current point is about 2.4 times the absolute IEA miscellaneous item and 3.8 times that
year's 0.66 mb/d interagency range.

## Scope and independence warnings

- EIA's series is petroleum and other liquids and includes categories such as biofuels and
  refinery processing gains. IEA and OPEC use total-oil/liquids conventions that IEF
  normalizes where possible, but calls on OPEC/DoC remain constructed residuals.
- OPEC's Table 10 balance is world demand less non-DoC liquids and DoC NGLs, compared with
  DoC crude production. It is algebraically a global balance plug but not a tank series.
- OECD commercial stocks are much narrower than IEA global observed stocks. Government
  reserves, non-OECD tanks and oil on water matter enormously in this episode.
- A statistical discrepancy is the supply-demand identity's closing item. A preliminary
  stock revision is a later change to an observed estimate. A mixed-vintage artifact is a
  project construction that combines months from different publications. They are related
  but not interchangeable.
- The agencies are not independent experiments. They use overlapping government
  submissions, JODI-type reporting, shipping intelligence and secondary-source production
  estimates, and later revise toward some of the same information. The three-way envelope
  is a model/vintage sensitivity, not a statistical confidence band.
