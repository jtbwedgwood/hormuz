# Refresh Hormuz transit data and set the current-traffic scenario baseline

## Description

Refresh the public PortWatch Hormuz series to the latest available date, quantify the June relaxation and latest stable traffic level, and define the provisional future-closure traffic assumption requested by the user.

## Acceptance Criteria

- PortWatch raw/external and derived tracker outputs are refreshed reproducibly through the latest available observation.
- The handoff states the exact data-as-of date and whether any final days are partial or subject to revision.
- Historical summaries preserve the June relaxation rather than treating 2026 as a constant closure.
- A defensible current-level statistic is recommended, with window and uncertainty, for holding traffic approximately constant through September, December, and March scenario horizons.
- Vessel calls/capacity are not presented as loaded oil volumes; mapping to oil supply remains a separate accounting step.

## Work Notes

- 2026-08-03: Started as part of the first `hormuz-m8q` parallel pass. This task owns refreshes to the existing PortWatch/tracker files and any new current-level scenario table. Coordinate around unrelated user changes and preserve existing tracker methodology.
- 2026-08-03: Refreshed the IMF PortWatch `Daily_Chokepoints_Data` ArcGIS layer using `.venv/bin/python scripts/fetch_portwatch_hormuz.py`. The local source and tracker now cover 2019-01-01 through **2026-07-23** (2,761 daily observations). Layer metadata report `dataLastEditDate=2026-07-28T12:39:23.266Z`. Thus the source's observed cutoff is July 23, not July month-end; on the August 3 research date it has an 11-calendar-day reporting lag.
- 2026-08-03: The ArcGIS layer has no published provisional/final flag at row level. The refresh revised 90 historical capacity fields, usually by one metric ton from rounding, and materially revised call counts/capacity for June 24-28. This establishes that the tail can be revised. Preserve the exact source vintage and refresh before publication; do not describe July 23 as final.
- 2026-08-03: June relaxation is preserved in observed history. June 24-July 7 averaged **31.86 total calls/day and 15.50 tanker calls/day** (446 total, 217 tanker calls over 14 days), versus 2019-2024 averages of 90.515 total and 54.827 tanker calls/day. This was 35.2% and 28.3% of the respective baselines. July 8 marks a clear return to a lower regime.
- 2026-08-03: Recommended provisional scenario baseline is the full **July 8-23** low-regime mean: **10.625 total calls/day** and **3.500 tanker calls/day**, or 11.7% and 6.4% of 2019-2024 baseline. It is stable across shorter windows: July 10-23 averages 10.286 total and 3.357 tanker; July 17-23 averages 11.143 total and 3.429 tanker. The scenario CSV gives approximate 95% sampling intervals of **9.351-11.899 total** and **2.772-4.228 tanker** calls/day. These are intervals for the mean under observed daily variation, not full measurement/political uncertainty.
- 2026-08-03: Added `data/derived/hormuz_m8q_2_current_traffic_scenario.csv`, generated by the existing tracker build script. It retains observed history through July 23 and holds the July 8-23 call rate constant from July 24 through the requested 2026-09-30, 2026-12-31, and 2027-03-31 horizons. It also has a separately labeled July 24-31 month-end nowcast so the oil-accounting work can run through July: central 85 total calls and 28 tanker calls, with sampling ranges 74.8-95.2 and 22.2-33.8.
- 2026-08-03: Interpretation guardrail: `n_tanker` is a count of broad-class chokepoint calls. PortWatch does not provide direction, loading status, oil/product type, cargo onboard, or AIS-dark movements. Neither tanker calls nor the noisy `capacity_tanker` proxy should be converted mechanically into mb/d. Use the traffic baseline to set timing/severity, then anchor oil volumes independently to IEA/EIA/cargo-flow estimates.
- 2026-08-03: Rebuilt the public tracker CSV, figure data, SVG, and blog widget through their established scripts; updated `data/manifest.csv` and `docs/hormuz-ship-tracker.md`. No dependency changes were needed.
