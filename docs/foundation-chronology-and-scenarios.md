# Foundation Chronology And Scenarios

Last updated: 2026-08-18. Scope: event chronology and baseline/scenario framing for `hormuz-fyp.2` and `hormuz-fyp.3`.

## Current Read

As of 2026-08-18, the June fragile reopening has failed, but "full closure" is still too blunt. Neutral transit remains officially permitted and some ships are crossing, while attacks, route control, naval checks and operator caution have reduced traffic to a small fraction of normal. The best-fit taxonomy is **S2 managed partial closure plus S1 security/insurance shock**, with S3 de facto closure as the downside sensitivity. AP reported 95 confirmed crossings in the latest week, down 19.5%, and only three on 16 August; all three used the Iranian-designated route and none used the Omani routes. The U.S.-Iran negotiating period ended without a settlement or announced extension. Use the August EIA STEO as the central energy-market baseline: severe constraints through August, slow flow increases beginning in September, most production and trade near pre-conflict patterns only in early 2027, and roughly 0.6 mb/d still disrupted at end-2027.

## Publication Wording Corrections

- Replace **"full closure resumed in July in response to U.S. violations of the memorandum"** with: **"The June reopening reversed after renewed attacks on ships and reciprocal U.S.-Iran escalation in July. Traffic fell back to a small fraction of normal, although some controlled transit continued."** UKMTO recorded attacks on 7 and 20 July; JMIC assessed the Strait at `SEVERE` on 14 July while saying neutral transit remained permitted. AP reports that Iran attacked vessels using the U.S.-overseen Omani route, the U.S. then struck Iran and restored its blockade, and each side accused the other of violating the memorandum. That evidence supports sequence and mutual breakdown, not sole U.S. causation.
- Source and date **"Iran recently issued maximalist demands"** as: **"In early August, Iran conditioned reopening on lifting the U.S. blockade, withdrawing U.S. forces from around Iran and paying war reparations, while insisting on an Iranian management role and possible fees."** AP reported the list on 17 August. "Maximalist" is analysis; the demands themselves should be stated.
- Replace the prospective **"60-day clock"** language with: **"The negotiating period expired on 17 August without a permanent agreement or announced extension."** The memorandum was signed 17 June; AP reported Pakistan treated Monday 17 August as the deadline and that no compromise on the Strait or detailed nuclear talks was evident. This resolves the one-day calendar ambiguity in a simple `signature date + 60 days` calculation by following the parties' reported operational deadline.

## Scenario Taxonomy

| Scenario | Definition | Observable triggers | Working assumptions | Key outputs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S0 Normal/recovered baseline | Traffic and flows return to pre-2026 patterns; war-risk premiums normalize. | Daily vessel counts within 90-110% of baseline for 30 days; no UKMTO substantial/high threat; no abnormal anchorage congestion. | Use as counterfactual for costs and tracker charts. | Normal daily ships, normal flow volumes, baseline prices/freight. |
| S1 Insurance/security shock | Strait remains legally open, but attacks, mines, GNSS interference, naval presence, or crew/insurer refusal reduces traffic and raises costs. | UKMTO warnings, war-risk premium spikes, rerouting south of TSS/Omani waters, anchorages growing, delayed voyages. | Physical supply may move with delays and higher freight; price impact concentrated in products, freight, and regional spreads. | Delay days, effective capacity haircut, insurance/freight adders, product-price passthrough. |
| S2 Partial closure/managed corridor | Some traffic allowed through preferred corridors, flags, cargoes, or negotiated windows; other vessels wait or reroute. | Confirmed crossings below 70% baseline; route selection shifts; official corridor/toll/control statements; persistent congestion. | Treat throughput as stochastic and politically constrained, not zero. | Daily allowed throughput, backlog, commodity priority ranking, importer exposure. |
| S3 De facto full closure | Commercial transits mostly stop because ship operators, insurers, or militaries deem passage too risky even if no formal legal closure is recognized. | Daily crossings near zero or only military/Iranian/special cases; P&I/war-risk withdrawal; large vessel queues on both sides. | Use 2026-02-28 onward early shock as template. Pipeline bypass and inventory draw become binding. | Supply removed/delayed, inventory draw, price shock, demand destruction. |
| S4 Formal closure/blockade | A state formally declares or enforces closure/blockade against broad vessel categories. | Official notices, interdictions/boardings, mine-laying confirmation, naval blockade enforcement. | Highest legal/geopolitical escalation; commercial data may degrade because AIS goes dark. | Maximum disruption case; separate verified transits from AIS-dark/unknown. |
| S5 Rerouting-delay adaptation | Operators avoid parts of Hormuz/TSS, wait offshore, use Saudi/UAE pipelines, or substitute non-Gulf supply. | Omani/southern corridor use, Red Sea/Yanbu/Fujairah flows, U.S. export surge, Asian replacement cargoes. | Does not remove all supply, but increases voyage times, freight, refinery mismatch, and product cracks. | Delay-adjusted barrels, route maps, substitution matrix. |
| S6 Fragile reopening | Diplomatic agreement creates partial recovery, but threat level and governance/toll disputes remain. | Rising crossings but below baseline; renewed warnings/attacks; toll or corridor disputes; output restoration announcements. | Historical best fit from 17 June to 7 July; **not current best fit as of 18 August**. Retain as an upside/recovery branch conditioned on a verifiable transit agreement, declining threat level and sustained crossings. | Recovery path, confidence bands, event-conditioned price scenarios. |

## Implementation Notes

- Day 0 for shock charts: 2026-02-28. For pre-event baselines, end windows on 2026-02-27.
- Treat EIA STEO as the central public energy-market source, not as ground truth. Label its figures as assumptions/forecasts when used for scenarios.
- Keep "traffic," "flow," "production shut-in," and "available supply" distinct. A ship-count drop is not mechanically equal to barrels removed because vessel size, cargo type, load status, and bypass/substitution matter.
- Use confidence labels: high = official/statistical source with clear definition; medium = reputable news or official statement reported secondhand; low = unsourced tracker/social snippet or inferred operational detail.
- Current largest uncertainties: actual daily transits by vessel class; AIS-dark traffic; exact war-risk insurance cost; unsold barrels versus delayed barrels; the terms and enforceability of any Iran-Oman traffic-management arrangement; whether U.S.-Iran negotiations resume after the 17 August deadline; and whether attacks or naval enforcement push the managed partial closure toward S3.
